The Button Who Steered the Coat

4 min read · ages 3–6 · bedtime story

Pip was a bright red button sewn to the very top of your child's winter coat.

He had four neat holes and four tight threads of yellow cotton.

Pip was extremely important, and he never let the other buttons forget it.

"The zipper is just the engine," Pip explained every morning. "The sleeves are the wings. But I—I am the steering wheel."

Down below, the middle button gave a tiny wobble. "I thought the child did the walking."

"Nonsense," said Pip. "Watch this."

your child put the coat on, zipped it up, and popped Pip securely into his buttonhole.

Click.

"Engine engaged," whispered Pip. "Prepare for departure."

Out the front door they went, into the crisp autumn wind.

When your child turned left toward the playground, Pip puffed out his chest.

"Hard left!" Pip shouted into the breeze. "Magnificent turn! Perfect navigation!"

They went past the bakery. The smell of warm cinnamon filled the air.

"Bypassing the bakery," Pip noted formally. "Staying on course. You may thank me later, stomach."

At the park, your child ran around the big oak tree three times.

"Slalom course!" Pip cried, thrilled. "Hold on to your threads! We are cornering at high speeds!"

The bottom button was hanging on for dear life. "Can we please go straight? I feel dizzy."

"No time for weakness!" said Pip. "Destination ahead: the slide!"

All afternoon, Pip steered past bicycles, around prams, and safely through the front gate.

When dusk settled over the street, the streetlamps flickered orange.

Inside the warm hallway, your child unbuttoned Pip with a gentle tug.

"Docking sequence initiated," Pip whispered, feeling the yellow threads relax.

The coat was hung smoothly on its wooden peg behind the front door.

Pip looked down the row of buttons in the quiet, dim hall.

"Another successful journey," Pip murmured, his shiny red face catching the nightlight.

"Good driving, Captain," whispered the middle button.

Pip smiled, tucked his threads in against the wool, and shut his four eyes for the night.

THE END.

One question for afterwards: Pip was very proud of his driving. What was your favourite adventure today?

Did you know? Buttons with holes were used for decoration for thousands of years before anyone thought to invent a buttonhole.
