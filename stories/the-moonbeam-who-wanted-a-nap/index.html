The Moonbeam Who Wanted a Nap

5 min read · ages 3–6 · bedtime story

High up in the night sky, while the stars were getting ready for work, a small silver moonbeam named Orlo was already yawning.

"I don't see why the sun gets the day shift," Orlo complained to the moon.

"The sun is very reliable," said the moon.

"The sun gets picnics and beach towels," said Orlo. "I get owls and empty car parks. And I am exhausted."

"You have responsibilities," the moon told him gently. "Go down there and shine on something noble."

Orlo slid down to Earth on a long, cool beam of light.

But Orlo had no intention of shining on anything noble. Orlo wanted a nap.

He tried sliding into the garden shed through the keyhole.

But the moment Orlo entered the shed, the watering can lit up like a disco ball, and three rakes cast long, dramatic shadows.

"Too bright," groaned Orlo. "I am ruining my own darkness."

Next, he tried sneaking under a large flat rock near the pond.

A toad looked at him in dismay. "Excuse me," said the toad. "I was sleeping. You are like a flashlight in my bedroom."

"Sorry," Orlo sighed, backing out.

Finally, Orlo drifted through an open window upstairs, right into your child's bedroom.

The room was quiet. A tiny nightlight hummed in the corner.

your child was lying in bed, blinking sleepily at the ceiling.

"Who is that on the rug?" your child whispered.

"It's me," said Orlo, curling in a silver ring by the wardrobe. "I am a moonbeam on strike. I need somewhere dark, but everywhere I go, I shine."

your child thought about this for a moment.

"Come over here," said your child.

Orlo crept across the wooden floorboards, climbing up the side of the bed until he rested on the blue quilt.

your child gently lifted the edge of the blanket and tucked Orlo right underneath, folding the dark wool over the silver light.

Under the quilt, the glow went soft and milky, like a warm glass of milk in the dark.

"Is that better?" whispered your child.

"It is perfection," breathed Orlo from beneath the blanket.

He dimmed his light down to a faint, gentle shimmer, not much brighter than a firefly.

In the quiet room, your child closed heavy eyes, while outside, the great silver moon smiled down upon the sleeping house.

THE END.

One question for afterwards: Orlo searched for a quiet, cozy spot to rest. What is your favourite thing about bedtime?

Did you know? Moonlight is actually sunlight bouncing off the rocky surface of the moon like a giant mirror.
