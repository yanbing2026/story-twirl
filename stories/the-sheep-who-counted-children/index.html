The Sheep Who Counted Children

4 min read · ages 3–6 · bedtime story

It was nearly bedtime in the green field at the top of the hill. The sun had gone soft and orange. The birds had stopped shouting and started whispering.

In the middle of that field stood one small sheep. Her name was Tilly. Tilly had one job, and everyone knew what it was.

"Count the children," said the old sheep. "That is what we do. A child lies in bed. You count. One child. Two children. Three children. And by the time you reach the sleepy ones, the child in the bed is fast asleep."

Tilly nodded. "Count the children. I can do that."

That night, Tilly stood by the fence and got ready. She took a deep breath. She looked at the sleeping houses. And then she counted.

"One CHILD," said Tilly, very loudly.

Nothing happened.

"Two CHILDREN," said Tilly, even more loudly.

Nothing happened. In a house at the bottom of the hill, a little girl sat up in bed and said, "Mum? I think there is a sheep shouting."

Tilly tried again. "THREE CHILDREN! FOUR CHILDREN! FIVE CHILDREN!"

Windows went on. Doors opened. A man in his pyjamas came outside and said, "Is everybody all right? Who is shouting numbers?"

"Me," said Tilly. "I am counting the children. It is my job. It is meant to make them sleepy."

The man in pyjamas looked at Tilly for a long moment. Then he said, "Have you tried counting something quieter?"

Tilly thought about this. She looked at the field. She looked at the sky. And then she saw them — small, soft, and moving very slowly across the dark: clouds.

"One cloud," said Tilly, quietly. "Two clouds. Three clouds."

In the house at the bottom of the hill, the little girl lay back down. The shouting had stopped. There was just a soft voice outside, going slowly past three, past four, past five.

"Six clouds," said Tilly. "Seven. Eight."

The little girl's eyes got heavy. "Nine clouds," said Tilly, and yawned. "Ten."

By eleven she had forgotten what she was counting. By twelve she was asleep with her chin on the fence, and the whole hill was quiet.

The old sheep found her there in the morning. "Did you count the children?" she asked.

"No," said Tilly. "I counted clouds. But everyone went to sleep anyway."

The old sheep thought about it for a while. Then she nodded. "Well," she said. "That is the same thing, only nicer."

And after that, every night, in that field, you could hear a small sheep going quietly through the sky: one cloud, two clouds, three — until the whole hill, sheep and children both, fell asleep together.

THE END.

One question for afterwards: Tilly tried one way, then another way. What is something you tried twice today?
