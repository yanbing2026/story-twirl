The Music Box in the Attic

4 min read · ages 3–6 · bedtime story

The attic smelled of boxes and old paper, and it was still warm up under the roof.

your child and Grandad went up at half past six with one torch. The ladder creaked, the hatch banged, and they stood in the blue evening.

There was a great deal up there: boxes, a bicycle, a chair with one arm, a lamp with no bulb, and a clock that had stopped.

They were after the blue box: the good lamp, the candles, and the jumper Grandad had once been far taller than. It was under the bicycle, which did not move, and neither did Grandad.

Then Grandad said, "Right. The box. And no, nothing up here works. Everything up here's finished. Nothing in this attic works any more."

your child put a hand out to point at the blue box, then took it back, because your child's own idea was to listen. Not to Grandad. To the attic.

And something was ticking on the floor, very small and out of step with itself, and coming from the smallest box, the one with a horse on the lid.

Grandad said, "That is nothing. It is the house settling. It does that."

But the noise was not above your child. It was below, and ticking things do not go on and off like that, and they both got down to look.

Inside was a small wooden box the colour of tea, with a horse on the lid and a metal handle.

Grandad said, "Leave that. It has not played since before your mother was born. There is no spring in it."

But your child turned the handle. Nothing happened. your child turned it again, and nothing happened at all.

Grandad said, "There. Finished." And he sat down on an old suitcase, the way you do when you have said what you came to say.

your child turned the handle a third time, all the way round, until it came back to where it started.

And the little drum inside began to turn. tick rattle, tick rattle, and a sound came out that neither of them had heard for years.

It was a tune: a small thin one with a wobble in the middle, getting slower, and slower, and the last notes hung in the air.

Grandad did not say anything. He sat on the suitcase with the torch up at the roof, and listened to it all.

"Well," he said. "It doesn't need a spring." "No," said your child. "It just needed turning."

And then it needed turning again, so your child turned it, and it played the same tune, and the window went blue to black.

They found the blue box in about a minute, with the jumper inside it, exactly where Grandad had said.

And at the bottom of the stairs the little box sat playing with the lid open, and the tune went down the corridor and up the stairs after them.

It was still going when your child was upstairs, and slower, and slower. Then it stopped in the middle of a bar, the way a song does.

Grandad carried it back up in the dark, and the hatch shut with a sound like a lid. In the morning the house was full of that same ticking.

THE END.

One question for afterwards: Is there something in your house that everybody says is finished, but it still works?

Did you know? Inside a music box there is a little drum covered in tiny pins. When you wind it, the drum turns slowly underneath a metal comb, and every pin flicks one tooth and makes exactly one note.
