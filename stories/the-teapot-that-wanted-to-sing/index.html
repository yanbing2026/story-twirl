The Teapot That Wanted to Sing

4 min read · ages 3–6 · bedtime story

On the middle shelf in the kitchen lived a round blue teapot named Arthur.

Arthur had a spout shaped like a trumpet and a handle like a fancy ear.

"Kettles only whistle," Arthur liked to say. "Whistling is vulgar. I, on the other hand, was built for opera."

The two white teacups beside him groaned.

"Please not the opera," said the big cup. "It is only Tuesday."

That evening, your child's grandfather filled Arthur with boiling water and three peppermint leaves.

As the water warmed his ceramic belly, Arthur felt the music rising.

"ME-ME-ME-MEEEE!" Arthur announced through his spout.

A high, wobbly blast of steam shot out and hit the toaster.

"Arthur," said the toaster, without looking up. "You are flat. And you are getting crumbs damp."

"That was just my warm-up," Arthur replied with great dignity.

He took another deep breath of steam. "LA-LA-LAAAAAA!"

The spoons in the drawer began to jingle. The salt shaker hopped half an inch to the left.

your child walked into the kitchen in bunny slippers, rubbing one eye.

"Who is making that funny squeak?" asked your child.

Arthur stopped. "A squeak? I am delivering a dramatic masterpiece."

"It sounds like a mouse blowing a party horn," said your child, smiling.

Arthur considered this. He looked at his reflection in the shiny kettle.

"A party horn?" he muttered, rather wounded.

"Bedtime needs quiet music," your child whispered, gently touching Arthur's warm handle. "Soft, like the rain."

Arthur let out a long, slow breath.

The steam didn't blast this time. It curled up in a lazy, quiet spiral towards the ceiling.

"Huuuummmmm," went Arthur, very gently. "Huuuum-shhhhh."

It sounded like the wind outside a cozy window, or a purring cat wrapped in a blanket.

The teacups settled happily into their saucers.

"Much better," murmured your child, taking the peppermint tea.

The kitchen lights clicked off, leaving Arthur on the shelf, humming the sleepiest note he knew until the whole house was fast asleep.

THE END.

One question for afterwards: The teapot learned that quiet songs are very special. What is your favourite quiet sound?

Did you know? The steam that comes out of a kettle is water turning from liquid into gas, which expands by over a thousand times.
