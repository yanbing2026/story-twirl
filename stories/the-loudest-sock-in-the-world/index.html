The Loudest Sock in the World

5 min read · ages 3–6 · bedtime story

On Monday, your child's opened the drawer and found a new sock. It was red, with one green stripe, and the moment the drawer opened, the sock said:

"HELLO!"

your child jumped.

"I AM THE LOUDEST SOCK IN THE WORLD," said the sock, happily. "AND I HAVE BEEN IN THIS DRAWER FOR SIX DAYS."

"Socks can't talk," said your child.

"WRONG," said the sock. "Socks can't talk QUIETLY. That is different, and it is my whole problem."

So your child put the sock on. It was warm. It was comfortable. It also shouted comments about everything it saw.

"WHAT A BIG KITCHEN," said the sock. "IS THAT THE KETTLE? I LIKE IT. I LIKE EVERYTHING HERE."

At breakfast, your child put the sock's foot under the table. "Please," whispered your child. "Please be quieter. People are looking."

"OKAY," said the sock. Then, a second later: "THE TOAST IS VERY GOOD."

At school, things got worse. During quiet reading, the sock said, "I CANNOT SEE THE PICTURES FROM DOWN HERE." During maths, the sock said, "NINE LOOKS LIKE A BALLOON." During the bit where everyone had to be completely silent, the sock said, in its biggest voice: "THIS IS MY FAVOURITE PART!"

Twenty-three children turned to look at your child's foot.

After school, your child sat on the front step and had a serious talk with the sock.

"You are getting me in trouble," said your child. "You have to learn to be quiet, or you have to live in the drawer."

The sock was quiet for a long time. So long that your child thought it had gone. Then it said, in a small voice:

"I don't know how."

your child thought about this. It is hard to know how to do something nobody has shown you.

"Right," said your child. "We'll practise. When I press your toe, that means quiet. When I squeeze your heel, that means you can talk."

"And nothing else?" said the sock.

"Nothing else," said your child.

They practised all evening. Press the toe — quiet. Squeeze the heel — "HELLO." Press the toe — quiet.

By bedtime, the sock could do it.

That night your child wore the sock to bed, which was unusual, but the sock was warm and had had a hard week.

"Squeeze or press?" whispered the sock.

"Squeeze," said your child.

The sock thought for a moment. Then it said, as quietly as it could — which for the loudest sock in the world was still not very quiet, but it was trying:

"Goodnight. I like you. I like everything here."

And your child fell asleep, warm, with one green stripe showing above the covers.

THE END.

One question for afterwards: The sock could not be quiet. How did your child help it?

Did you know? Your feet have about a quarter of all the bones in your body — 52 of them, and your toes do half the work of balancing.
