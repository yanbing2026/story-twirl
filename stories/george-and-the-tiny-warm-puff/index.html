George and the Tiny Warm Puff

5 min read · ages 3–6 · bedtime story

George was a dragon, and he wanted everyone to remember it.

He had emerald green scales, two pointy wings the size of maple leaves, and a tail that ended in a sharp little triangle.

There was only one problem: George was the size of a teacup, and he had a secret he considered deeply humiliating.

In the evenings, George would sit on the hearth rug, plant his tiny claws, and practice his ferocious roar.

"BEHOLD THE TERROR OF THE MOUNTAIN!" George shouted.

He sucked in a huge breath of air. His scales rippled. His eyes narrowed.

POOF.

A small, pale cloud of smoke floated out of his nostrils.

It didn't smell like burning logs or scorched stone. It smelled exactly, undeniably, like hot buttered toast.

"Unacceptable," grumbled George, fanning the cloud away with his tail. "Simply embarrassing."

your child sat down on the rug cross-legged, holding a bedtime storybook.

"I think it smells lovely," your child said.

"I am a beast of ancient legend," George said stiffly. "I am not a breakfast appliance."

"Try again," encouraged your child. "Maybe aim for the chimney."

George puffed his chest until he looked like a round green tennis ball.

"FLEE BEFORE MY WRATH!" George cried.

Puff.

Another warm little cloud drifted out, this time smelling faintly of cinnamon.

George slumped onto his belly and buried his snout in his paws.

"Ruined," he sighed. "A dragon who belongs in a bakery."

The room was getting chilly as the night wind tapped at the windowpane.

your child pulled the thick woolen blanket down over cold feet and gave a little shiver.

George peeked out from behind his paws. "Are your toes freezing?"

"A bit," your child admitted.

George crept over, curled himself into a neat green doughnut right across your child's slippers, and let out three slow, gentle puffs.

The blanket grew delightfully warm, cozy and soft.

"See?" whispered your child, pulling the covers up to the chin. "Fierce dragons could never do that."

George rested his chin on his tail, purred a sleepy rumble that smelled of warm bread, and closed his eyes.

THE END.

One question for afterwards: George was sad he could not breathe fire, but his toast puff was even better. What makes you feel cozy?

Did you know? Many lizards and reptiles like to bask on warm rocks because they rely on outside warmth to stay cozy.
