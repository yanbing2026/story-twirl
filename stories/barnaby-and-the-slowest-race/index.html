Barnaby and the Slowest Race

4 min read · ages 3–6 · bedtime story

In the vegetable patch behind the kitchen, Barnaby the snail was polishing his shell with a drop of morning dew.

Barnaby was not an ordinary snail. Barnaby was built for pure racing.

"Look at these curves," Barnaby said to a nearby worm. "Aerodynamic. Crisp. Unstoppable."

The worm poked its head out of the soil, blinked once, and went back down.

"Jealousy," said Barnaby, nodding wisely. "A common reaction to greatness."

That afternoon, your child sat on the warm stone step, tying up a red sneaker.

Barnaby slid onto the gravel path and drew a line in the dust with his tail.

"You. In the red shoes," announced Barnaby. "Race me to the flowerpot. If you dare."

your child looked down at the tiny brown spiral on the path.

"The big flowerpot?" said your child. "That is six steps away."

"Six steps for an amateur," Barnaby scoffed. "A grand tour for a professional. Three, two, one—GO!"

your child took six steps and reached the flowerpot in four seconds.

Then your child turned around and sat down to watch.

Barnaby was already moving. He was giving it everything he had.

A leaf blew past him. Then an ant carrying half a potato chip overtook him on the inside lane.

"Drafting," Barnaby muttered between his teeth. "Very clever ant. But he cannot hold that pace."

By tea-time, Barnaby had passed the first pebble.

By bath-time, Barnaby was halfway past a dry twig, leaving a sparkling silver ribbon behind him.

"Look at that acceleration," Barnaby whispered to a sleeping beetle. "I am practically flying."

When your child came outside in pyjamas to say goodnight, the garden had turned purple and blue.

Barnaby had finally arrived at the edge of the pot.

"A photo finish," wheezed Barnaby happily. "I think I took it by a whisker."

your child gently placed a fresh, cool lettuce leaf right next to him.

Barnaby took one tiny bite, yawned a snail-sized yawn, and pulled his head into his shell.

"Engine cooling down," came a muffled voice from inside the shell. "Rematch tomorrow. After breakfast."

And the garden went quiet, under the silver path of the moon.

THE END.

One question for afterwards: Barnaby was very proud of his speed. What is something you took your time doing today?

Did you know? A garden snail's top speed is about one metre an hour, and they can sleep for days at a time.
